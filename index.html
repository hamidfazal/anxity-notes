1. Introduction & Terminology Overview

Anxiety is a normal adaptive response to a perceived threat. However, it becomes clinical pathology when it meets specific maladaptive thresholds.

Pathological Anxiety Markers
Excessive • Persistent • Difficult to control • Disproportionate to the situation • Functionally impairing
Term Clinical Meaning
Anxiety Normal human emotion or transient clinical symptom.
Anxiety Disorder A diagnostic cluster of psychiatric illnesses characterized by excessive fear and anxiety.
GAD A chronic, persistent disorder characterized by excessive, multi-domain worry.
Panic Attack An acute, sudden, discrete episode of intense fear peaking within minutes.
Panic Disorder Recurrent, unexpected panic attacks accompanied by persistent anticipatory anxiety.

Fear vs. Anxiety

Feature Fear Anxiety
Trigger Response to an immediate, definite threat Anticipation of a future, vague threat
Temporal Profile Acute Chronic / persistent
Somatic Driver Fight-or-flight dominant (sympathetic blast) Hypervigilance and tension dominant

2. DSM-5 Anxiety Disorders Classification & Neurobiology

According to the DSM-5-TR, the major anxiety disorders cluster includes:

  • Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)
  • Panic Disorder
  • Social Anxiety Disorder (SAD)
  • Specific Phobias
  • Agoraphobia
  • Separation Anxiety Disorder
  • Selective Mutism

Pathophysiology

Understanding the neurocircuitry is critical for board exams and explaining pharmacological mechanisms to patients:

  • Key Brain Regions Involved: Amygdala (threat processing), Prefrontal Cortex (impaired top-down regulation), Hippocampus (contextual conditioning), and Locus Coeruleus (norepinephrine surge).
  • Neurotransmitter Dysregulation: Decreased Serotonin (5-HT) and GABA; elevated Norepinephrine (NE). Glutamate and Dopamine are also implicated in modulatory roles.

3. Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)

Characterized by persistent, uncontrollable, multi-domain worry occurring more days than not across a prolonged period.

DSM-5-TR Diagnostic Criteria

  1. Excessive anxiety and worry occurring more days than not for at least ≥ 6 months, spanning multiple domains (e.g., work, health, family, finances, daily chores).
  2. The individual finds it difficult to control the worry.
  3. Associated with ≥ 3 of the following 6 somatic/cognitive symptoms (only 1 item is required in pediatric patients):
Diagnostic Mnemonic: “RFCIMS”
  • R – Restlessness or feeling keyed up/on edge
  • F – Fatigue (easily exhausted)
  • C – Concentration difficulties or mind going blank
  • I – Irritability
  • M – Muscle tension (prominent physical feature)
  • S – Sleep disturbance (onset insomnia, restless sleep)
  1. The symptoms cause clinically significant distress or functional impairment.
  2. The disturbance is not attributable to physiological effects of a substance or medical condition.

Clinical Presentation Overlap

Psychological Elements

  • Persistent worry & catastrophizing
  • Overthinking & cognitive locked loops
  • Compulsive reassurance seeking

Somatic & Behavioral

  • Tension headaches & muscle stiffness
  • Autonomic hyperarousal (GI upset, palpitations)
  • Avoidance and procrastination
Typical Patient Presentation Note
“Doctor, my brain never stops. I worry about my health, my kids, and mistakes at work. Even small things stress me out and I can't turn it off.”

4. Panic Attack vs. Panic Disorder

A crucial exam and clinical distinction must be maintained between a panic attack (a transient event) and panic disorder (a syndromic condition).

Panic Attack Definition & Criteria

An abrupt surge of intense fear or intense discomfort that peaks within minutes, during which ≥ 4 of the following symptoms occur:

  • Palpitations, pounding heart
  • Sweating, trembling, shaking
  • Shortness of breath / choking sensations
  • Chest pain or discomfort
  • Nausea or abdominal distress
  • Dizziness, unsteadiness, or faintness
  • Chills or heat sensations
  • Paresthesias (numbness/tingling)
  • Derealization or Depersonalization
  • Fear of losing control or “going crazy”
  • Fear of dying

Panic Disorder Criteria

Diagnosis requires recurrent, unexpected panic attacks followed by ≥ 1 month of one or both of the following:

  • Persistent concern or worry about additional panic attacks or their consequences (e.g., losing control, having a heart attack).
  • A significant maladaptive change in behavior related to the attacks (e.g., avoidance of exercise, shopping malls, or public transit to prevent an attack).

Head-to-Head Comparison

Feature Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) Panic Disorder
Core Feature Chronic, free-floating, pervasive worry Recurrent, sudden-onset autonomic terror
Onset & Profile Gradual onset; continuous across months Sudden paroxysmal onset; peaks in <10 mins
Primary Cognitive Fear Failure, health of loved ones, day-to-day errors Fear of imminent death, stroke, or going crazy
Somatic Signpost Prominent skeletal muscle tension Severe autonomic storm (palpitations, dyspnea)

5. Medical & Psychiatric Differentials

Anxiety and panic are diagnoses of exclusion. Somatic mimicry must always be thoroughly evaluated before assigning a psychiatric etiology.

Organic Medical Differentials

  • Hyperthyroidism (Check TSH/Free T4)
  • Cardiac Arrhythmias (e.g., SVT)
  • Pheochromocytoma (Episodic HTN/sweats)
  • Hypoglycemia
  • Pulmonary Embolism / Asthma
  • Substance/Caffeine Intoxication

Psychiatric Differentials

  • Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)
  • Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)
  • Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
  • Illness Anxiety Disorder

6. Comprehensive Management Principles

Non-Pharmacological (First-Line)

  • Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT): Cognitive restructuring to target catastrophizing, combined with systemic exposure protocols for panic-related avoidant behaviors.
  • Adjunctive Interventions: Applied relaxation, structured sleep hygiene tracking, diaphragmatic breathing metrics, and aerobic exercise.

Pharmacological Intervention Strategy

Line of Therapy Drug Classes & Examples Clinical Notes & Key Strategies
First-Line (Long-term) SSRIs: Escitalopram, Sertraline, Paroxetine
SNRIs: Venlafaxine, Duloxetine		 Delayed onset (4–6 weeks). Start at half the typical depressive dose to minimize transient initial activation/anxiety spikes.
Second-Line / Adjuncts Buspirone (GAD only)
Pregabalin / Hydroxyzine		 Buspirone requires scheduled dosing (not PRN) and does not possess immediate efficacy or sedative properties.
Acute / Short-Term Benzodiazepines: Clonazepam, Alprazolam Use with strict limitations to avoid dependence, tolerance, cognitive blunting, and withdrawal syndromes. Useful as bridging therapy.
High-Yield Exam Pearls
  • Panic Attacks inside other conditions: Panic attacks can happen in PTSD, MDD, and phobias. Panic Disorder specifically requires unexpected attacks paired with ≥1 month of anticipatory dread or behavioral avoidance.
  • Scale Metrics: Use GAD-7 tracking tools for objectivity in clinical trials and field tracking logistics.
